What the Ha Giang Loop actually costs

By Matthew Lee · Last updated: August 2026

A guided trip with me runs $190 to $549 depending on length and style, all inclusive. Doing it yourself costs less upfront — bike rental is $7 to $21 a day — but food, fuel and lodging are on top and add up faster than people expect.

What's included in a guided price

Accommodation, meals, water, the bike (self-drive) or the bike and rider (easy rider), and on the longer trips the bus ticket back to Hanoi. You are not pricing those separately once you book — the number above is the number you pay.

Self-drive costs, going entirely on your own

Bike rental in Ha Giang City runs from a Honda Wave 110cc at around $7 a day to a Honda XR 150cc at $21 a day — see the full rental price table for the models in between. That is only the bike. Here is a realistic daily budget for the rest, per person:

ItemPer day
Food~300,000 VND (~$12)
Petrol~100,000 VND (~$4)
Homestay or hotel room500,000–1,000,000 VND (~$19–38)
Bike rental$7–21 (Wave to XR — see the full table)

All in, that is roughly $42–75 a day per person depending on which bike and which room, on top of transport to and from Ha Giang City itself.

Costs people forget

Parts of this route sit close enough to the Chinese border to need a travel permit — on my trips I arrange that as part of booking. If you are self-driving independently, you will need to sort this yourself before you set off; ask at your hotel in Ha Giang City or a local police station, since this is exactly the kind of detail that changes without much notice online.

Where this guide is wrong for you

If your only goal is the lowest possible number, self-driving in a larger group splitting fuel and rooms will usually beat any guided price I can offer. I am not going to pretend otherwise.

Which is actually cheaper

For a solo traveller or a pair, a guided trip is often close to self-drive once you add up independent food, fuel, permit hassle and the cost of any mistakes — a wrong turn, a night in the wrong town, a bike that breaks down without backup. For a larger group splitting costs, self-drive is usually genuinely cheaper. Neither answer is universally right, which is why I would rather you ask me your specific numbers than trust a generic comparison.
